▸ Outlets × classes

Shows only the outlets currently checked under "Media / source" above. Articles = distinct documents linked via your CSV's rawnb. Segments = the number of Iramuteq text-segments those articles were split into — that comes from Iramuteq itself, not the CSV, and can differ from article count if an outlet writes longer or shorter pieces. The per-class numbers are an index: 100 means an outlet's segments cover that class exactly as often as the corpus shown here does; well above 100 means it over-indexes on that theme. Outlets with fewer than 5 segments are left out to avoid noise from single-article outlets.

▸ Possible shared/syndicated coverage

Groups of near-identical headlines published by different outlets the same day — a lead worth checking for wire-copy or syndicated stories, not a certainty (this compares titles only, not full article text).

Coverage by class

Segments — pick a period

Segments are ranked by the sum of χ² (Iramuteq's own specificity statistic, from profiles.csv) of this class's characteristic words that actually appear in each segment's text — the most characteristic segments come first, not database order. Each matched word's own p-value is shown (e.g. "p<0.0001" means the association is extremely unlikely to be chance; "p=0.32 (not significant)" means it isn't a reliable signal) so you can judge significance directly rather than trusting a raw χ² number. Word matching is a simple substring check on the lemma forms Iramuteq already uses, so it can occasionally over-match inside a longer word.
